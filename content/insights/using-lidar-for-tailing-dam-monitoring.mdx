**Executive Summary:**

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) is increasingly vital for the precise, real-time monitoring of **tailings dams**—engineered embankments that store mining waste—by offering high-resolution, three-dimensional surface data. The traditional, labor-intensive approaches to tailings dam surveillance, often reliant on periodic visual inspections and ground-based instruments, have struggled to detect early-stage deformations or subsidence with sufficient spatial and temporal coverage. LiDAR addresses these limitations by delivering rapid, remotely-sensed data that enable continuous monitoring and detailed change detection across extensive, hazardous, or inaccessible areas. Integrating LiDAR with other remote sensing modalities (e.g., multispectral, thermal) and data management platforms substantially improves risk assessment, supports proactive maintenance, and enhances safety for both workers and surrounding communities.

Emerging research and industry deployments show LiDAR’s capabilities in monitoring topography, detecting subtle surface movement, and supporting volume calculation for both tailings and water, optimizing operational efficiency and compliance with international standards like the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM). Recent case studies demonstrate successful synergies with AI/ML for anomaly detection and automation with IoT infrastructure—yielding productivity gains and earlier warnings of potential failures. However, challenges remain, particularly around data processing, cost, and the need to combine LiDAR data with traditional monitoring for comprehensive safety outcomes. As LiDAR technology becomes more accessible and integrated, the future of tailings dam monitoring is shifting toward holistic, data-driven strategies, with increasing regulatory, operational, and reputational benefits.

**Challenges & Future Trends:**

- **Challenges:**
  - **High upfront costs** for LiDAR hardware and data processing infrastructure[6].
  - **Data management complexity**: Need for advanced analytics to handle and interpret large LiDAR datasets[2].
  - **Limited value when used in isolation**: Optimal results achieved when combined with traditional, in situ sensors and geotechnical modeling[2][1].
  - **Skill gap** in remote sensing data interpretation and lack of standardization in protocols[4].
