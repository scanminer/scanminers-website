## Executive Summary

Cobalt plays a crucial role in the global battery supply chain, particularly for electric vehicles and renewable energy technologies. As demand for these technologies increases, so does the need for responsible cobalt sourcing and exploration. Remote sensing technologies, such as hyperspectral and synthetic aperture radar (SAR), are increasingly important for locating cobalt deposits, monitoring artisanal mining activities, and ensuring environmental, social, and governance (ESG) compliance. In 2023, global cobalt demand grew by 10% year-over-year, driven mainly by battery chemistries like mid and high nickel NCM[1]. Challenges in the cobalt supply chain include risks associated with artisanal mining, market volatility, and the need for diversified supply sources.

Key contributors to the cobalt market include China, which dominates the global trade of battery minerals, and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), which supplies a significant portion of the world's cobalt[2]. As the energy transition accelerates, maintaining a stable and sustainable cobalt supply chain is critical for supporting the growth of electric vehicles and renewable energy systems.

## Key Data Points
- **Global Cobalt Supply**: Rose by 17% in 2023, with the DRC maintaining a 76% market share[1].
- **Cobalt Demand**: 55% of total battery demand in 2023 was for cobalt-containing chemistries, despite growth in cobalt-free LFP[1].
- **China's Role**: Imported 20% of the world's processed battery minerals and exported 58% of battery materials in 2023[2].
- **Indonesia's Growth**: Became the second-largest cobalt producer with an 86% increase in output in 2023[1].
- **Artisanal Mining Impact**: Impacted by weak cobalt prices and increasing global mined supply[3].
