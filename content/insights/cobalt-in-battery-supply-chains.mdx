# Cobalt in Battery Supply Chains

## Executive Summary

Cobalt remains a critical mineral in the global battery supply chain, with 76% of production concentrated in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) as of 2023[1]. The metal's importance stems from its use in lithium-ion batteries, particularly in electric vehicles (EVs) and portable electronics, which collectively account for 76% of total cobalt demand[1]. In 2023, global cobalt supply increased by 17% year-over-year while demand grew by 10%, creating market dynamics shaped by rapid production expansion at facilities like CMOC's Kisanfu mine, which alone added 32,500 metric tons of supply[1]. China dominates the processing stage, handling over 90% of the world's graphite processing and controlling two-thirds of global cobalt and lithium processing capacity[2]. Mid-nickel NCM (nickel-cobalt-manganese) chemistries overtook lithium cobalt oxide (LCO) as the primary driver of battery cobalt demand in 2023, accounting for 48% of battery sector consumption[1]. Despite the rise of cobalt-free lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries, cobalt-containing chemistries still represented 55% of total battery demand in 2023, with this share expected to remain steady in the medium to long term[1]. The supply chain faces significant challenges including geographic concentration risks, artisanal and small-scale mining (ASM) concerns, and the need for responsible sourcing practices. Remote sensing technologies, including hyperspectral imaging, synthetic aperture radar (SAR), and optical systems, are increasingly being deployed to locate cobalt-bearing deposits, monitor mining activities, and support environmental, social, and governance (ESG) compliance efforts across the supply chain.

## Recent Findings & Case Studies

### CMOC's Kisanfu Mine Expansion (2023)

The commissioning and rapid ramp-up of CMOC's Kisanfu mine in the DRC represented a pivotal development in 2023 cobalt supply dynamics. The mine exceeded market expectations by adding 32,500 metric tons of supply in its first year of operation[1]. Combined with production from Tenke Fungurume, CMOC increased total output by 35,000 metric tons, surpassing Glencore to become the world's largest cobalt producer for the first time[1]. This case demonstrates how single-project developments can significantly impact global supply balances and shift competitive dynamics among major producers. The Kisanfu expansion contributed to the DRC supporting 78% of annual supply growth in 2023 while maintaining its dominant 76% market share[1].

### Indonesia's HPAL Operations and Supply Chain Diversification (2023)

Indonesia's emergence as a major cobalt producer through high-pressure acid leach (HPAL) operations processing nickel and cobalt marked a significant shift in supply chain geography. Indonesian cobalt output increased 86% year-over-year in 2023, with the country solidifying its position as the second-largest producer[1]. The pipeline of HPAL facilities continues to expand as Indonesia pursues a strategy to capture greater market share in the battery supply chain[1]. Projections indicate that by 2030, Indonesia will account for 16% of global cobalt supply, up from 7% in 2023, while the DRC's share is expected to decline to 67%[1]. Between 2023 and 2030, Indonesia is forecast to support 37% of total supply growth, second only to the DRC's 48%[1]. This case illustrates ongoing supply chain diversification efforts aimed at reducing dependence on a single geographic source.

### Mid-Nickel NCM Chemistry Shift (2023)

The battery chemistry landscape underwent a significant transformation in 2023 as mid-nickel NCM formulations overtook lithium cobalt oxide (LCO) as the primary driver of cobalt battery demand. Mid-nickel chemistries experienced 15% year-over-year growth and came to represent 48% of cobalt demand from the battery sector, pulling ahead of LCO at 37%[1]. High-nickel chemistries also gained ground with 32% year-over-year growth, reaching an 11% market share[1]. This shift was driven by the electric vehicle sector gaining market share relative to portable electronics, which use predominantly LCO chemistries[1]. The finding demonstrates how evolving battery chemistry preferences directly impact cobalt demand patterns and supply chain planning requirements. Despite the growth of cobalt-free LFP batteries, which reached 413 GWh of demand in 2023, cobalt-containing chemistries maintained a 55% share of total battery demand[1].

### China's Battery Mineral Trade Dominance (2023)

China's position across the battery supply chain strengthened in 2023, with the country accounting for 44% of interregional raw and processed battery mineral imports (almost 12 million short tons) and 58% of battery materials, packs, and components exports (almost 11 million short tons)[2]. Australia, the world's largest lithium producer, sent almost all its exports to China alone[2]. China domestically produced 79% of the world's natural graphite in 2024 and 18% of mined lithium in 2023[2]. Chinese companies also control 25% of global lithium mining capacity and have significant investments in Argentina's lithium triangle, which contains 50% of the world's lithium reserves[2]. This case illustrates the concentration of processing capabilities and the strategic importance of mineral trade relationships in the battery supply chain.

### Aerospace and Defense Sector Support (2023)

The aerospace sector continued its faster-than-expected post-Covid recovery in 2023, supporting cobalt demand in superalloy applications, which represent cobalt's largest non-battery end use at 9% of total demand[1]. Global military spending reached a new record in 2023, further supporting cobalt use in niche defense and aerospace applications[1]. This case highlights the diversity of cobalt demand beyond the battery sector and the resilience of traditional industrial applications even as battery demand dominates growth trajectories.

## Challenges & Future Trends

### Geographic Concentration and Supply Chain Risks

The cobalt supply chain faces significant vulnerability from extreme geographic concentration, with 76% of production originating from the DRC and a single producer (CMOC) holding substantial market power after surpassing Glencore in 2023[1]. Chinese companies control 80% of cobalt production in the DRC, creating additional geopolitical risk layers[2]. While Indonesia's emergence as the second-largest producer offers some diversification, the supply chain remains highly concentrated. By 2030, projections indicate the DRC's share will decline to 67% as Indonesia reaches 16%, but this still represents significant concentration risk[1]. The dominance of China in processing—handling over 90% of graphite and two-thirds of cobalt processing capacity—compounds supply chain vulnerabilities[2].

### Artisanal and Small-Scale Mining (ASM) Concerns

Artisanal and small-scale mining has lost market share due to weak cobalt prices and increasing global mined supply from large-scale operations[3]. However, ASM remains a critical responsible sourcing concern due to labor practices, environmental impacts, and traceability challenges. Remote sensing technologies offer potential solutions for monitoring ASM activities, detecting unauthorized mining sites, and supporting ESG compliance efforts. The integration of hyperspectral imaging, SAR, and optical remote sensing can enable more effective surveillance of mining areas and help identify unauthorized extraction activities that may compromise responsible sourcing commitments.

### Battery Chemistry Evolution and Demand Uncertainty

The battery chemistry landscape continues to evolve, creating uncertainty in long-term cobalt demand projections. While cobalt-containing chemistries maintained a 55% share of battery demand in 2023, cobalt-free LFP batteries are gaining significant ground, particularly in the Chinese market[1]. The automotive industry's pivot toward plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) and hybrid vehicles in the mid-term, with reduced emphasis on battery electric vehicle (BEV) sales, may alter cobalt intensity assumptions[1]. High and mid-nickel chemistries showed strong growth at 32% and 15% year-over-year respectively in 2023, while low-nickel and LCO chemistries declined[1]. The trajectory of chemistry preferences will significantly impact future cobalt requirements.

### Inventory Dynamics and Market Volatility

The 2023 market experienced significant inventory management challenges as battery supply chains began the year with considerable raw material stockpiles accumulated in 2022 due to overstocking in anticipation of higher EV demand[1]. When growth underperformed expectations—partially due to the removal of Chinese EV incentives at the end of 2022 and broader macroeconomic conditions including high interest rates—the industry underwent supply chain-wide destocking[1]. Cell manufacturers reduced capacity utilization, leading to decreased spot market purchasing of cobalt for much of 2023[1]. This pattern highlights the challenge of demand forecasting and inventory optimization in a rapidly evolving market.

### Remote Sensing Applications for Responsible Sourcing

The application of remote sensing technologies presents opportunities to address supply chain challenges through improved mineral exploration, mining activity monitoring, and ESG compliance verification. Hyperspectral imaging can identify cobalt-bearing deposits and mineralized zones by detecting specific spectral signatures associated with cobalt mineralization. Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) enables all-weather monitoring of mining activities, infrastructure development, and land disturbance patterns regardless of cloud cover—particularly valuable in tropical regions like the DRC. Optical remote sensing provides high-resolution imagery for tracking mining site expansion, detecting unauthorized ASM activities, and monitoring environmental impacts including deforestation and water pollution. The integration of these technologies with artificial intelligence and machine learning algorithms can automate detection of mining activities and support continuous monitoring programs for responsible sourcing initiatives.

### Processing Capacity and Value Chain Control

China's dominant position in mineral processing creates bottlenecks and strategic vulnerabilities for consuming regions seeking to develop independent battery supply chains. The United States produced no natural graphite in 2024, and China's control of two-thirds of cobalt and lithium processing capacity positions the country as a critical intermediary between raw material producers and battery manufacturers[2]. Efforts to develop processing capacity outside China, including through the U.S. Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, Inflation Reduction Act, and CHIPS Act investments, represent attempts to diversify processing capabilities[6]. However, the capital intensity and technical expertise required for battery mineral processing create substantial barriers to rapid diversification.

### Market Balance and Price Dynamics

The 2023 cobalt market experienced its largest surplus in volume terms, driven by supply growth (17% year-over-year) outpacing demand growth (10% year-over-year)[1][3]. The Kisanfu mine's production exceeding market expectations contributed significantly to the surplus[3]. While the price decline slowed in 2023 compared to prior years, persistent oversupply conditions present challenges for producers, particularly smaller operations and ASM that cannot compete at lower price points[3]. Future market balance will depend on the pace of EV adoption, battery chemistry evolution, and the commissioning timeline of planned production capacity, particularly Indonesia's expanding HPAL operations.
